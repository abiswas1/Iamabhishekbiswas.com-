01 · The challenge

Setting up the tool had become a project of its own.

Before teams could use Terraform Enterprise to manage their servers, networks and storage, someone had to install it, secure it and keep it running.

The managed service changes that arrangement. Each customer gets their own environment, while IBM keeps it running, updated and backed up. Our task was to make that change clear from setup through to everyday use.

01 · The tool

Terraform

Engineers describe the servers, networks and storage they need in code. Terraform creates or changes them consistently.

02 · The team platform

Terraform Enterprise

Terraform Enterprise (TFE) helps teams review changes, set rules and control access. They organise this work in workspaces. With the usual installation, the customer also maintains the platform.

03 · The managed service

TFE on IBM Cloud

IBM maintains the platform. Customer teams use it to manage their own servers, networks and storage. Each customer gets a separate environment, called an instance.

The design challenge: help teams understand what they need to set up, what IBM takes care of and how to start their work.

02 · My contribution

Leading the experience from setup to first use

As Lead Product Designer, I worked with product, engineering, architecture and service reliability teams to define how customers would set up and start using Terraform Enterprise on IBM Cloud.

The challenge was to bring requirements around licensing, networking, security and access into a journey customers could follow.

  1. 01

    Define the customer journey

    Mapped the steps from setting up an instance to signing in and starting work in Terraform Enterprise. Identified where customers needed guidance, what IBM would manage and where the experience moved between the two products.

  2. 02

    Work through technical constraints

    Worked with engineering and product teams to understand service requirements, permissions and credentials. Used those constraints to shape the setup flow while keeping it consistent with familiar IBM Cloud patterns.

  3. 03

    Design beyond the successful setup

    Created task flows, wireframes and detailed screens for provisioning and instance management. Covered empty accounts, failed setup, licence changes and deletion, alongside the main journey.

  4. 04

    Turn research into design priorities

    Tested the prototype with three experienced Terraform users. Their difficulties with login details, navigation and service responsibilities gave us specific issues to address in design reviews and the next iteration.

  5. 05

    Explore the next version with AI

    Used Figma Make to turn ideas for Phase 2 into an interactive prototype, covering guided setup, workspaces, access and policy checks. This helped make the proposed experience easier to explore before development.

    AI supported the screen building and interactions. My design focus remained on what customers needed to understand, which actions they could take and when they would move from IBM Cloud into Terraform Enterprise.

05 · Phase 1

A clear route from setup to first use.

Phase 1 focused on getting a customer from an existing TFE licence to a working instance and their first task. We followed familiar IBM Cloud patterns, then looked closely at the points where people could become unsure.

Once the instance is ready, customers need to know how to sign in and start using Terraform Enterprise.

01

Use familiar setup steps. Explain the licence.

The form follows the usual IBM Cloud setup steps: location, plan, licence and instance details. Help beside the licence field explains what is needed. Customers can also use a key they have stored securely.

Why this approach

Customers should be able to set up this service using the IBM Cloud steps they already know.

What the first version includes

Customers bring their own licence and use one standard plan. Other licence options and plan upgrades are not part of the first setup flow.

What testing revealed

Participants understood the licence requirement. The wording around access still needed to be clearer.

02

Show what is ready and who looks after it.

The Overview brings status, instance details and the button to open TFE into one place. We also showed the services IBM manages behind the scenes. Testing challenged that choice: one participant questioned why they needed to see those services, while another expected to change them.

The design needs to show enough detail to help customers trust the service, while making clear which tasks IBM handles.

03

Help customers start their first task.

Getting Started was meant to help customers use the instance they had just created. But one participant selected “create your first workspace” expecting TFE to open, and reached documentation instead. The label promised an action that the link did not deliver.

Make the sign in steps clear. Label instructions separately from actions that open Terraform Enterprise.

Help people when things change or go wrong.

The design also covers an empty account, failed setup, licence updates and deletion. Each screen explains what has happened and what the customer can do next.

07 · Phase 2 / The advanced version

From getting started to managing everyday work.

Phase 1 helped people get started. The advanced version extends that experience into everyday work, from checking service health to managing workspaces, access and rules. Figma Make helped us connect these workflows in a prototype and examine how they would work together.

Try the interactive prototype ↗

What AI sped up

Turning ideas into something we could try.

Figma Make helped connect screens, create example states and make the steps interactive. The team could explore a complete flow and see where it needed more thought before development.

What still needs a designer’s judgement

Deciding what the product should do.

  1. Explain who does what. IBM maintains the platform. Customers set up workspaces, rules and team access in TFE. A company name on a badge is not enough to explain that difference.
  2. Choose where each task belongs. Customers can check workspace status in IBM Cloud, but need a clear link to TFE to make changes.
  3. Check permissions and rules. Product and engineering teams need to check how access and rules work across both products, followed by user testing. A working prototype does not prove the real systems will connect correctly.

08 · Impact

Faster setup today. Easier everyday work next.

The first result was a shorter setup. The next goal is to make the service easier to work with after that first login, so teams can check progress, find problems and understand what to do next.

Phase 1 · Tested result

Instance setup went from days to 1 to 2 hours.

The managed service reduced setup time in testing. Usability sessions also showed where login details and the split between IBM’s tasks and the customer’s tasks needed to be clearer.

Phase 2 · Expected impact

What the advanced version could improve.

These are the intended benefits if Phase 2 is implemented. They still need to be checked through user testing and after release.

Less time checking different places

A shared view of instances, workspaces and recent activity could help teams find the status they need with less switching between products.

Earlier awareness of problems

Monitoring and alerts could help teams notice service issues sooner and see what needs attention.

Fewer avoidable mistakes

Setup review and policy checks could help teams catch missing details or blocked changes before they continue.

Clearer access and responsibilities

Access mapping and clear labels could help people understand what they can change, what their team manages and what IBM handles.

How we would check the impactMeasure time to find service status, successful task completion, avoidable setup errors and requests for help with access.

Reflection

What changed, and what we learnt

The project made setup faster. Testing helped us understand what people still needed before they could start using the service with confidence.

  1. 01

    What worked

    Instance setup went from days to 1 to 2 hours in testing. Bringing the setup into a managed service reduced the work customers needed to do before they could begin using Terraform Enterprise.

  2. 02

    What needed more attention

    People still struggled to find their login details and understand what IBM would manage. Getting the service running was one milestone. Helping someone sign in, find their next task and understand their responsibilities needed equal attention.

  3. 03

    What I take forward

    Figma Make helped turn the advanced version into a prototype we could explore and discuss. AI made it easier to connect the screens, but deciding where tasks belonged and how access should work still needed design judgement and collaboration with product and engineering.

A simpler experience should leave people clear about their next step, where to take it and what they are responsible for.
