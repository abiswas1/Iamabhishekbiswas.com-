Case study / Cogoport 3.0 shipment execution

Making shipments easier to run.

Once a container is booked, the hard part begins. Documents, approvals and updates pass between the shipper, freight forwarder, customs agent and trucker, usually by phone, email and WhatsApp. In five months, our team designed Cogoport's first mobile app and a web experience that brought all of that into one place.

The project in 20 seconds
Problem
After booking, shipment updates, documents and responsibilities were scattered across channels.
My role
Senior Product Designer across research, process mapping, prototypes and usability testing.
Key decision
Make each shipment the starting point for its status, assigned tasks and documents.
Outcome
The team reported 24–48 hours saved on document filing and escalation, and 1,500+ containers in the first month.
Role
Senior Product Designer: research, process mapping, wireframes, prototyping and usability testing
Team
1 product owner, 2 product managers, 2 UX designers, 1 UI designer and interns
Timeline
5 months, launched November 2019
Platforms
Mobile app (Cogoport's first) and web

Context

After the booking, things go wrong.

After the booking, a shipment still moves through many people, documents and deadlines. Our research showed that coordination was where customers lost time and confidence.

Users

Designing for the people who make the calls.

We designed for owners who wanted someone to handle the shipment and managers who needed control over costs and tasks.

Process mapping

Mapping every checkpoint.

We mapped 29 export and 25 import checkpoints, then marked the responsible stakeholder and the steps the product could support.

Testing

Show the shipment’s status and next task on the card.

In testing, people did not recognise the home-screen cards as their active shipments. They could miss the work waiting for them before even opening a shipment.

What testing exposed

This was the highest-severity issue in our review. The card needed to make the shipment recognisable and show whether anything needed attention.

What we changed

We brought the route, shipment status and next task together on the card. People could see which shipment they were looking at and what to do next.

Final design

What we shipped.

The revised card became the entry point to a shipment. The screens below show how status, tasks and documents connect to it.

Everything at a glance

02 / Tasks

Everyone knows what's theirs

03 / Invoices and documents

Clear costs, secure files

Invoices show every line item, so shippers see exactly what they're paying for.

Results

What changed.

Cogoport 3.0 launched in November 2019. The team reported gains in adoption and retention, less time spent on documentation and escalation, and more than 1,500 containers booked and shipped in the first month. These are outcomes of the wider Cogoport 3.0 release; my focus was the shipment execution experience.

Looking back

What this project taught me.

Testing changed a central assumption: a shipment card only helps if people recognise it as their live work. The first release also needed room to evolve as customers adjusted from familiar offline habits.
