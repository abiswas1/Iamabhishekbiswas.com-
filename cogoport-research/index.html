Case study / Cogoport 3.0 research

Investigating why growth in a freight marketplace slowed.

Cogoport launched its digital marketplace for ocean freight in 2017 and grew fast. By 2018, that growth was slowing. Before redesigning anything, our team needed to understand why. I interviewed founders and internal teams, led the Mumbai field study, and helped the team turn the findings into priorities for Cogoport 3.0.

The project in 20 seconds

Growth was slowing, and teams had different assumptions about who used the marketplace.

Context

Early growth slowed in 2018.

Cogoport’s freight marketplace grew quickly after launch. By 2018, growth had slowed, and the team needed to understand why before redesigning the product.

Step 1 / The business

Leadership and internal teams disagreed about who used the product.

I asked founders and teams what the product was for and who it served. Leadership described owners as the main users. Internal teams said employees and Cogoport staff often handled bookings because owners found the product difficult to use.

Step 2 / The market

Demand was growing, supply wasn't.

The marketplace needed freight suppliers, but some saw it as a threat to their customer relationships. More demand alone would not solve that imbalance.

Step 3 / The users

Sitting with shippers.

The field study involved five freight buyers across Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and Baroda over two days. An associate product manager and I led the Mumbai research team. The five roles below are the parties involved in a shipment. They are not five separate groups of research participants.

Step 4 / Synthesis

Grouping research findings by shipment stage.

Back together, we grouped observations by Plan, Enquire, Booking and Execution. The affinity map helped the team separate repeated behaviour from isolated requests.

Findings

What we learned.

  1. Trust matters more than price

    Every shipment is a large investment, so exporters stick with agents they know. A slightly cheaper rate from a stranger isn't worth the risk.

  2. Comparing agents is part of the job

    Shippers check 3 to 4 agents on every shipment, because each is strong on different routes. The product had to support that habit, not fight it.

  3. Shipping isn't their business

    For most small and medium businesses, logistics is a cost to manage, not where they make money. In about half of them there's no export manager, so the owner or someone else handles it on the side.

  4. Documents cause the most worry

    A lost or leaked document can cost a client, and exporters still need paper records for government checks. Their main goal is to hand the goods to the agent and get the bill of lading on time.

  5. Suppliers need a reason to join

    Without forwarders and agents on the platform, there's nothing to book. They needed to see Cogoport as a partner, not a competitor.

Outcome

Support trusted agents, secure documents and reliable updates.

A competitive rate was only part of the decision. Shippers also needed confidence in the agent, control over their documents and reliable updates once a shipment was booked.

What we heard

Shippers relied on agents they knew. They worried about sensitive documents and coordinated shipments through repeated calls and messages. A booking tool alone left that work unresolved.

Priorities for Cogoport 3.0

We prioritised business verification, document sharing and a single place for shipment updates. Clear costs during planning remained important, alongside these needs.

My role I led the Mumbai field study with an associate product manager and contributed to stakeholder research and the team’s synthesis.

  • Verified registration, so only genuine businesses could see rates. This protected suppliers and Cogoport.
  • Secure, shared documents for everyone involved in a shipment.
  • One channel for updates, instead of calls, emails and messages to each party.
  • Clear, standardised costs during planning, so decisions could be made faster.
Looking back

What this project taught me.

The research showed that competitive rates alone were not enough. Shippers relied on trusted agents, worried about document security and needed help after booking. These findings gave the team specific priorities for the redesign.
