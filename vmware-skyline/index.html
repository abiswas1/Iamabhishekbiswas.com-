Case study / VMware Skyline

Proactive health checks for VMware customers.

VMware's Technical Account Managers check the health of their customers' infrastructure and recommend improvements. That work was manual, slow and inconsistent. I designed the experience in Skyline that turned it into a guided, largely automated report, from activating the service to sharing the final PDF.

Role
Product Designer: user journeys, wireframes and final UI
Team
3 product designers, working with product, front-end and QA
Timeline
5 months
Product
VMware Skyline Advisor and Skyline Viewer

To comply with my non-disclosure agreement, I have omitted and obfuscated confidential information. This case study is my own and doesn't necessarily reflect the views of VMware or Broadcom.

Context

What a health check is.

Skyline gathers infrastructure findings that a Technical Account Manager (TAM) can use in a customer health check. Before this project, turning those findings into a report relied on manual work and several handoffs.

Users

Three people, one report.

Anita activates the service as an administrator. Anil, the TAM, prepares the health check. Paul in Professional Services can create a report when a customer requests one.

Journey

Mapping the handoffs first.

Activation starts in Skyline Advisor and finishes in Skyline Viewer. I mapped what each person could see and do before designing the screens.

Design

From activation to a finished report.

01 / Activation

Two clear steps for the customer

The customer sees two steps and can copy the organisation ID for the TAM.

02 / Report creation

Start from what Skyline already found

Triggered best-practice findings are included by default. The TAM reviews grades and adds observations before previewing the report.

03 / The report

Readable by the customer, not just the expert

The report brings an executive summary, severity heatmap and detailed findings into one exportable view.

04 / Report library

Every report in one place

The library keeps completed reports and data exports together for the entitled roles.

Delivery

Seeing it through to release.

I worked with product, frontend and QA through handoff and user acceptance testing. Access across the three roles produced the most edge cases.

Looking back

What this project taught me.

Mapping the handoffs early made the report flow easier to design and test across roles.
